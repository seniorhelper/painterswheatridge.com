Old cabinets are often the good ones

A 1960s Wheat Ridge kitchen frequently has solid wood face frames, real plywood boxes and doors that still hang square after sixty years. Mid-range replacement cabinetry does not match that construction, and it will not be here in 2086.

What is actually dated is the finish: orange-toned stain, yellowed varnish, or a honey oak refresh somebody did in 1994. That is a coating problem, and coating problems are fixable for a fraction of replacement cost.

Why cabinet paint chips, every time

When somebody calls us about cabinets that chipped within a year, the cause is always one of four shortcuts, and usually two.

The first is grease. Six decades of frying sits in a film on every door within reach of the stove, and it is invisible until you wipe one with solvent. Primer cannot grip through it.

The second is sanding, or rather not sanding. Old varnish and factory finishes were engineered to be slick so food wipes off them. That same slickness is exactly what a coating needs to overcome, and the only way to do that is to abrade it.

The third is the primer itself. Ordinary wall primer is the wrong tool. A bonding primer is formulated to hold onto an already-cured finish, and on old varnished oak it does a second job by sealing in tannins that would otherwise bleed through.

The fourth is the topcoat. Wall paint stays slightly soft forever. A cabinet door closes a hundred times a week against a frame, and soft paint loses that argument.

Tannin bleed, which old wood does

Older oak and some other woods release tannins that migrate up through a light-colored coating and leave yellow-brown staining, sometimes weeks after the job looks finished. It is a real risk on vintage cabinetry and the fix is a stain-blocking bonding primer rather than hoping.

A painter who does not mention this when quoting white paint over old varnished oak has not thought about it.

Working around a compact kitchen

Mid-century kitchens are small, which means we cannot build a spray booth in the corner. Doors and drawer fronts come off, get labeled and leave. Boxes are sprayed in place behind containment, and you keep your counters and appliances throughout.

Questions

Cabinet painting questions

How much does cabinet painting cost in Wheat Ridge?

Published Front Range data puts professional cabinet refinishing at roughly $2,000 to $8,000 for a typical kitchen. Door and drawer count and the condition of the existing finish drive the number, and mid-century kitchens are often smaller, which helps.

Will old varnished wood bleed through white paint?

It can. Older oak and some other woods release tannins that migrate up through light coatings and leave yellow-brown staining. A stain-blocking bonding primer prevents it, and any painter quoting white over old varnish should mention this.

Are 1960s cabinets worth keeping?

Frequently yes. Solid wood frames and real plywood boxes from that era often outclass mid-range replacement cabinetry. What is dated is usually the finish rather than the construction.

Do you spray or brush?

Spray. Doors and drawer fronts are finished off-site and boxes are sprayed in place behind containment. Brushed cabinets look brushed, which defeats the purpose.

How long is my kitchen out of action?

Three to five days on site. You keep countertops and appliances throughout, and doors are off for most of that, so plan on open shelving for the week.

Can you fix a cabinet job that chipped?

Usually. We assess whether the existing coating can be sanded and recoated or has to come back to bare, then rebuild the finish properly.
