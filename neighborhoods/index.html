Applewood

Large lots and custom homes on the west side toward Golden, many with mature landscaping.

  • Big lots mean more fence, more outbuildings and far more exterior surface than the floor plan suggests.
  • Irrigation on large properties regularly soaks the lowest siding courses, which is the most common cause of early peeling here.
  • Mature trees shade north elevations and leave west walls in full sun, so one house often needs two approaches.

Fruitdale & Ridge Road

The historic agricultural heart of Wheat Ridge, with farmhouses and early-century homes.

  • Most of this housing predates 1978, so federally required lead-safe work practices apply to any disturbed painted surface.
  • Original wood siding and deep trim detail take far more hours per wall than modern construction.
  • Old outbuildings, barns and sheds are common and are usually quoted alongside the house.

Paramount Heights

Post-war ranches on the southwest side near the Lakewood line.

  • 1950s and 60s brick-and-frame combinations where each material needs its own preparation.
  • Original wood fascia and soffit on these homes reward real scraping, sanding and priming.
  • Kitchens of the same vintage usually have solid cabinet boxes, which makes refinishing a strong-value project.

Wheat Ridge Highlands

Established mid-century streets in the center of town.

  • Long low rooflines and generous trim runs that add surface area most estimates underprice.
  • Cedar privacy fencing from the original builds is common and is usually stained with the house.
  • Steady family ownership means more whole-house interior projects than quick cosmetic refreshes.

Clear Creek Corridor

Properties along the greenbelt and Clear Creek, including larger parcels.

  • Shaded elevations near the creek hold moisture and can grow mildew that must be cleaned, not painted over.
  • Larger parcels frequently include shops, barns and long fence runs.
  • Flood-plain-adjacent properties sometimes have moisture history worth diagnosing before any coating goes on.

38th Avenue & Ridge at 38

The main street corridor with small commercial frontage and the homes behind it.

  • Storefront and small-office repaints scheduled around business hours and sidewalk traffic.
  • Mixed-use blocks mean coordinating with both residents and businesses on the same street.
  • Older commercial buildings often carry layered paint that needs scraping back to a sound edge.

Prospect Valley

North-central Wheat Ridge, mostly 1960s and 70s housing stock.

  • Split-levels and ranches with original hardboard siding that needs inspection before painting.
  • Swollen or soft siding edges have taken on water and need more than a coat of paint.
  • Covenant rules are rare here, which keeps color choice simple.

Lutheran & West 32nd

Neighborhoods around the hospital campus and the 32nd Avenue corridor.

  • A mix of rentals and owner-occupied homes, so turnover work and full repaints sit side by side.
  • Compact lots with limited staging room require careful containment and masking.
  • Interior repaints are frequent here as long-held homes change hands.

Straight answers

Wheat Ridge neighborhood questions

Which Wheat Ridge neighborhoods do you paint?

All of them, including Belmar, Green Mountain, Applewood, Solterra, Kendrick Lake, Bear Creek, Eiber, Molholm, Glennon Heights, Rooney Valley, Wheat Ridge Estates and the Villa Italia corridor.

Do Wheat Ridge neighborhoods require HOA color approval?

Several of the master-planned communities do. Check your covenants, and we will match approved colors, prepare samples and handle the submission so paperwork never delays the crew.

Are older downtown homes different to paint?

Yes. Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint, which means federally required lead-safe work practices, and the deep trim detail on those houses takes far more hours per wall.

Do you work on several homes in one neighborhood?

Often. When neighbors book together we can run a street in one mobilization, which usually lowers everyone's cost.
